# Topics with Subscriptions; the Worker is the consumer group; fan-out comes from the architecture

For the Events chapter the game adds one building, the **Topic**, modelled on Apache Kafka. A Topic keeps a Backlog for each **Subscription**, so every subscribing system gets its own copy of each Message (pub/sub), while a Message Queue (ADR-0006) still gives each Message to one Worker. We did not add a separate consumer group building: the existing **Worker** gets a setting, *Subscription*, chosen from the level's list, and each Worker building connected to a Topic is one consumer group. Its instances share the Messages as they already do on a queue, but only as many instances as the Topic has **Partitions** can work at once; the rest wait. This is the real Kafka rule and it needs no new building, so the lesson stays on the idea, not on new pieces.

Each Subscription has its own **Consumer Lag** (in seconds: how long its oldest unprocessed Message has waited) and its own maximum in the level, judged like the Queue Age. We use the Kafka name because it is the word used in interviews; Kafka often counts it in Messages, which the Manual explains. A Subscription with a loose maximum (analytics) can fall behind without slowing one with a tight maximum (notifications), which is the main reason teams use pub/sub.

The **Partition Key** spreads Messages over Partitions with the same skew rule as the Shard Key (ADR-0007), so a poor key makes a **Hot Partition**. We do not simulate ordering: in real systems the key exists mostly to keep one user's events in order, but out-of-order errors would be hard to show on the map; the Manual explains it. Partitions and the key change only in the Build Phase.

The news feed uses the same pieces. A **Feed** Subscription Worker writes each post once per follower into the Database (*fan-out on write*); with no Feed Worker, each `read feed` reads the posts of every followed account (*fan-out on read*). The player picks the strategy by building it or not, not with a switch. A **Celebrity Limit** on the Feed Worker skips fan-out for accounts above the limit and merges their posts at read time (the hybrid model Twitter uses).

## Consequences

- Levels of chapters 1 to 4 keep working unchanged: they have no Topic, and a Worker without a Subscription behaves as today.
- `post` is answered when it enters the Topic (as a job entering the Message Queue). Users reach a Topic only through an app server. A Topic cannot send to a Message Queue (the SNS + SQS pattern is in the Manual only), so there is one way to fan out.
- Work released by a Topic is not the users' any more: it does not count in p95, errors or money. Only the Feed Worker writes to the Database; the other Subscriptions end at their Worker, because their real destinations (push service, search index, data warehouse) are outside the system.
- The flow model gains one Backlog and one Consumer Lag per Subscription, kept between steps like the Backlog of ADR-0006. A level that allows a Topic must set a maximum Consumer Lag for each Subscription, so the Drain Phase ends; it runs until every Subscription is empty.
- When a Worker instance goes down, the other instances of its group take its Partitions after a short pause (a rebalance). No Message is lost.
- The glossary test that forbids "partition key" in code (it guarded the Shard Key) must allow the new Partition Key.
- Retention and replay, exactly-once, dead-letter topics, retries and adding Partitions during the wave are not modelled; the Manual mentions them.

## As built

- **Topic and Subscriptions (level 27)**: `post` and `readFeed` are Request Types. The Topic is a station with the `pubSub` primitive (fixed price $30, `distributes`, users refused with `topicNotPublic`). A level lists its Subscriptions in `play.subscriptions` with a `maxLagS` each; a level that allows a Topic, or whose wave has posts, must list them. A Worker has the param `subscription` (catalog field `subscribes`); it finishes posts itself (catalog field `ends`), so a Notifications, Search or Analytics Worker needs no way to the Database. Partitions are not built yet: every serving Worker instance works.
- **What a Subscription owes**: each Subscription's Backlog grows with every post answered anywhere (entering the Topic, entering a Message Queue, or finished straight at a Worker) and shrinks with the posts the Workers serving that Subscription process. So a Message Queue, which gives each post to one Worker, leaves every Subscription missing the posts the others took: their Backlog grows and never drains, which is how level 27 loses with a queue. This is a simplification: in real life those posts are not late but lost for that system; the game shows the loss as a lag that never ends. For this the Message Queue also accepts posts, and carries the type its wave sends it.
- **Consumer Lag** is the Backlog over the rate the Subscription's serving Workers can take, or grows with the clock when none can (like the Queue Age). Reputation falls as for a slow base while any Subscription is over its maximum. The Drain Phase lasts until every Subscription's Backlog is empty.
- **One Worker building per Subscription**: in a level with Subscriptions the Worker card builds a new building for the first Subscription without one (refused with `allSubscribed` once all have one); more instances come from the Worker's inspector. The Subscription can be changed in the Build Phase only (`subscriptionLocked`), like the Shard Key.
- **Partitions (level 29)**: the Topic has the param `partitions` (catalog field `partitions`: 2 by default, $20 plus $5 for each, so the default Topic still costs $30 and levels 27 and 28 are unchanged; at most `componentOptions.topic.limits.partitions`, 12 by default). Partition p goes to working instance p mod n of each Subscription, and a Subscription uses at most as many instances as there are Partitions; the others are drawn as "idle · no Partition". Each instance processes its Partitions' share up to its capacity, and the Subscription's rate (for its Consumer Lag) is the throughput at which its busiest instance is full. Partitions change only in the Build Phase (`partitionsLocked`). The old event engine's `designCost` prices Partitions the same way.
- **Partition Key and Hot Partition (level 30)**: a level offers `play.partitionKeys` (weights per key value, the first key is the default), and the Topic has the param `partitionKey`, set in the Build Phase only (`partitionKeyLocked`). Value v goes to Partition v mod n, as for Shards. Each Subscription keeps a Backlog in each Partition (`partBacklog`, `topicId|subscription`), like a Kafka consumer group's offset per Partition: a post waits in its own Partition, so the instances of cool Partitions cannot empty a hot one. The Consumer Lag is the time the slowest instance needs to empty the Partitions it reads. The Hot Partition uses the Hot Shard rule (the busiest Partition is full while another has room) and is marked on the Topic and its load bar.
- **News feed (level 31)**: a level sets `play.feed` (the Subscription that writes the feeds, an account's followers, the accounts a user follows). A Worker of that Subscription, fed posts and connected to a building with the catalog field `feedStore` (the database), writes each post as `followers` background `feedWrite` requests (an internal Request Type, never the users' own); then a `readFeed` costs the database one read (fan-out on write). With no such Worker, a `readFeed` costs one read per followed account (fan-out on read). The Feed Worker connects itself to the database; the other Subscriptions' Workers do not. The cache also accepts feed reads (a timeline cache), for the review; level 31 does not offer one, so a cache cannot hide the cost of fan-out on read there.
- **Celebrity Limit (level 32)**: `play.feed.celebrity` sets the celebrity's moment (`atS`, `lengthS`), the `share` of posts that are theirs, their `followers` (3,000 in the game: real celebrities have millions, but the game's database serves hundreds of requests a second) and how many celebrities a user follows (`followed`). The Worker has the param `celebrityLimit` (catalog field: 10, 100, 1,000 or 10,000 followers; none by default), free and changeable at any time like the TTL. Posts of accounts above the limit are not fanned out; each feed read then merges `followed` more reads (the hybrid feed). A limit under a normal account's followers fans out nobody, so the feed falls back to fan-out on read.
- **Rebalance**: when the incident takes down an instance of a Worker that serves a Subscription, its whole group stops for `REBALANCE_S` (2 s), during which no instance reads; then the remaining instances share the Partitions again. An idle spare instance (more instances than Partitions) takes over the fallen one's Partition, which is the N+1 lesson for consumer groups.
- **The review (level 32)**: 500 requests a second (40% invalid, 2% posts, 58% feed reads): an API Gateway at the door, a load balancer and two app servers, a cache with a TTL up to 120 s for the feed reads, a Topic with three Partitions, four Notifications instances (one spare), three Feed instances with a Celebrity Limit of 1,000. The celebrity posts from 20 s to 40 s; a Notifications instance goes down at 45 s. A design without the spare still survives, with a lower reputation: the review checks the Celebrity Limit, not every lesson.
- **Look**: variant A of the prototype, without the Partition bars (they come with Partitions). The Topic shows its total Backlog; each Worker shows its Subscription in its title, a Consumer Lag gauge and an exit label ("→ push service"); the top bar shows the Subscription closest to its maximum; the console's first chart is the Consumer Lag of every Subscription as a share of its own maximum.
